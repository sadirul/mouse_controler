Touchless desktop control

Your hand is the mouse.

Hand Gesture Mouse watches your hand through a normal webcam and turns finger movements into real Windows mouse actions: move the pointer with your index finger, pinch to click, drag files, scroll pages, move and arrange windows - all without touching anything.

Install in 5 minutes Learn the gestures

What it does

Controls the real system cursor, clicks, double-clicks, right-clicks, long-presses, drag & drop, vertical and horizontal scrolling, and window management (move, minimize, maximize, restore, snap, switch, close).

Built for safety

An open palm stops everything instantly. Pause from the dashboard, the tray or a global hotkey. Closing a window always needs confirmation, and system windows are protected.

Private & offline

Video never leaves your computer. Hand tracking runs locally with Google MediaPipe; no account, no internet connection and no cloud service is needed.

Under the hood

How hand tracking works

Every camera frame goes through a small pipeline. It runs at about 25-30 frames per second on a typical laptop, on a background thread so the window always stays responsive.

1 · WebcamA frame is captured (and mirrored so your movements feel natural).
2 · MediaPipe21 landmarks per hand: wrist, every joint and every fingertip.
3 · Finger statesEach finger is extended, partially bent or folded; pinch distances are measured in hand sizes.
4 · Gesture engineA debounced state machine decides the gesture, so gestures never conflict.
5 · Your computerSmoothed cursor movement, clicks, scrolling and window commands.

Why the cursor is steady

Raw fingertip positions jitter by a few pixels. A One Euro filter smooths the cursor when your hand is still but stays fast when you move quickly, a small dead zone ignores micro-movements, and while your thumb approaches your index finger the cursor slows down so clicks land exactly where you aimed.

Why gestures don't collide

The gesture engine is always in exactly one state. While you drag, clicks and scrolling are ignored; while you scroll, the cursor stays still. A pose must be held for a short, configurable time before it counts, and every action has a cooldown. The emergency-stop palm is checked before anything else.

Installation guide

Install and run

Using the ready-made Windows build? Skip steps 1-4: unzip HandGestureMouse anywhere and double-click HandGestureMouse.exe. Python is not needed.

  1. Install Python

    Download Python 3.11 or newer from python.org. In the installer tick “Add python.exe to PATH”. Check it worked in a new terminal:

    python --version

  2. Download the project

    Download and unzip the project, or clone it with Git, then open a terminal in the project folder:

    cd hand-gesture-mouse

  3. Create a virtual environment

    This keeps the project's packages separate from the rest of your system.

    python -m venv .venv
.venv\Scripts\activate

    PowerShell refuses to run the activate script? Run Set-ExecutionPolicy -Scope CurrentUser RemoteSigned once, or use Command Prompt.

  4. Install the dependencies

    OpenCV, MediaPipe, NumPy, PyAutoGUI, PyGetWindow, pynput and PySide6:

    pip install -r requirements.txt

    The hand-tracking model (models/hand_landmarker.task) ships with the project. If it is missing, download it once:

    curl -L -o models\hand_landmarker.task https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-models/hand_landmarker/hand_landmarker/float16/latest/hand_landmarker.task

  5. Connect a webcam

    Plug in a USB webcam or use the built-in one. Close other apps that might use the camera (Teams, Zoom, the Camera app). Sit so your hand is clearly visible about 40-80 cm from the camera, with light on your hand.

  6. Run the application

    python main.py

    Options: --minimized starts in the system tray, --start-tracking starts tracking immediately, --self-test checks camera, model, tracking speed and the gesture engine (without moving your mouse) and shows a report.

  7. Allow camera access

    If the camera does not open, enable Settings → Privacy & security → Camera → Let desktop apps access your camera in Windows. The dashboard shows a clear message when access is blocked.

  8. Start gesture tracking

    Press Start tracking on the dashboard. You will see your hand with its 21 landmarks in the preview. Raise your index finger and the cursor follows. For safety, tracking never starts automatically unless you enable that in Settings.

Quick start

Your first minute

1. Find the box

The preview shows a grey “active area”. Your fingertip inside that box covers the whole screen, so small hand movements are enough.

2. Point

Raise only your index finger. Move slowly at first - the cursor follows smoothly.

3. Pinch

Tap your thumb tip against your index fingertip and open again. That is one left click.

4. Know the brake

Show your open palm for 1.5 s, press Ctrl+Alt+X, or click Emergency stop. Everything stops.

Tip: make a fist to “lift the mouse”: the cursor stays where it is while you move your hand back to a comfortable position.
How to use

Gesture-by-gesture tutorial

Each animation shows the fingers involved on the left and what happens on your screen on the right. Use Pause, Replay and 0.5× to study a movement. Highlighted fingers are the ones that matter; the cyan dot is your index fingertip (the cursor), the orange dot your thumb tip.

1 Move the cursor Index finger

Steps
  1. Raise your index finger; fold the other fingers (or let them relax).
  2. Keep your hand inside the active area shown in the preview.
  3. Move the fingertip - the cursor follows left, right, up and down.
Computer action
Moves the mouse cursor
Common mistakes
  • Middle finger also raised - that is the scroll gesture, so the cursor stops.
  • Hand too close to the frame edge, where tracking is less accurate.
Tips
  • Make a fist to freeze the cursor and re-centre your hand.
  • Too fast or too slow? Change Cursor speed on the Cursor page.

2 Single left click Thumb + index pinch

Steps
  1. Point at the target.
  2. Touch your thumb tip to your index fingertip.
  3. Open the pinch again.
Computer action
One left click where the pinch started
Common mistakes
  • Pinching very slowly while moving - the cursor slows down near a pinch, but large hand movement during the pinch becomes a drag.
  • Touching the side of the finger instead of the tip.
Tips
  • Holding the pinch never repeats the click.
  • The click is sent after the double-click window (350 ms) so a double click is never split into two singles. Disable double click if you want instant clicks.

3 Double click Two quick pinches

Steps
  1. Point at a file or folder.
  2. Pinch and release twice quickly (within 350 ms by default).
Computer action
Double left click (opens files and folders)
Common mistakes
  • Pausing too long between pinches - you get two single clicks instead.
  • Moving the hand away between pinches.
Tips
  • The cursor is held still between the two pinches so both land on the same spot.
  • Raise Max time between pinches if your double clicks are not recognised.

4 Right click Thumb + middle pinch

Steps
  1. Point at the item.
  2. Touch your thumb tip to your middle fingertip.
  3. Release.
Computer action
One right click (context menu)
Common mistakes
  • Thumb also touching the index finger - that counts as a left pinch.
  • A tight fist: folded fingers never count as a pinch, by design.
Tips
  • Lift the index finger slightly out of the way.
  • A 400 ms cooldown prevents accidental repeats.

5 Long press Hold the pinch

Steps
  1. Pinch thumb and index finger.
  2. Keep holding without moving for 0.8 s - a ring fills up in the preview.
  3. Release when done.
Computer action
Holds the left button down (or right click - your choice)
Common mistakes
  • Moving during the hold starts a drag instead (which also holds the button).
Tips
  • Choose Long press action on the Gestures page: “press and hold” (drag activation) or “right click”.
  • The duration is adjustable from 0.3 to 3 seconds.

6 Drag and drop Pinch, move, release

Steps
  1. Point at a file, icon, text or window title bar.
  2. Pinch and keep pinching.
  3. Move your hand - the item follows.
  4. Release the pinch to drop.
Computer action
Button down → move → button up
Common mistakes
  • Opening the pinch mid-way drops the item early.
  • Hand leaving the camera view - the item is dropped after 0.25 s for safety.
Tips
  • While dragging, motion follows your knuckles, not your fingertip, so the item doesn't jump when you pinch.
  • Clicks and scrolling are disabled during a drag.

7 Vertical scroll Two fingers up / down

Steps
  1. Point at the page you want to scroll.
  2. Raise index and middle fingers held together (others folded).
  3. Move both fingers up to scroll up, down to scroll down.
Computer action
Mouse-wheel scrolling (smooth)
Common mistakes
  • Spreading the fingers into a “V” - deliberately ignored to avoid accidents.
  • Expecting the cursor to move - it stays put while scrolling.
Tips
  • Change Scroll speed, or switch to Joystick style: hold the fingers above or below the start point to keep scrolling.

8 Horizontal scroll Two fingers left / right

Steps
  1. Make the same two-finger pose.
  2. Move your hand left or right.
Computer action
Horizontal wheel scrolling
Common mistakes
  • Starting diagonally: the first clear direction locks the axis until you release the pose.
Tips
  • Works in browsers, spreadsheets, timelines and file lists; some older apps ignore horizontal scrolling.

9 Move a window Three fingers

Steps
  1. Click the window you want to move (it becomes the active window).
  2. Raise index, middle and ring fingers and hold for ¼ s - the window is grabbed.
  3. Move your hand; the window follows. Change pose to release it.
Computer action
Moves the active window, keeping its size
Common mistakes
  • Pinky also raised - that is the four-finger swipe pose.
  • Trying to move an app running as administrator (see troubleshooting).
Tips
  • Maximized windows are restored automatically when grabbed.
  • Prefer to grab the window under the cursor, or drag via the title bar? Both are options.

10 Window swipes Four fingers, thumb tucked

Steps
  1. Raise four fingers with your thumb folded across the palm.
  2. Swipe quickly (under ⅔ s) in one direction.
Computer action (defaults)
Up: maximize · Down: restore or minimize · Left/Right: snap to half screen
Common mistakes
  • Thumb sticking out: that is the open palm (emergency stop when held).
  • Moving slowly - only quick swipes count, so you can relax your hand safely.
Tips
  • Every direction can be remapped (minimize, maximize, restore, snap, move to top, switch app...).

11 Switch application Horns hold

Steps
  1. Raise index and pinky; fold middle and ring fingers.
  2. Hold for 0.6 s.
Computer action (default)
Alt + Tab - switch to the previous app
Tips
  • Can be remapped to any window command.

12 Close a window Thumb + pinky, twice

Steps
  1. Touch thumb and pinky tips and hold 0.7 s - “Close armed” appears.
  2. Release, then repeat the hold within 4 s to confirm.
  3. Do nothing to cancel.
Computer action (default)
Closes the active window (asks to save if the app does)
Safety
  • Never closes the desktop, taskbar or Hand Gesture Mouse itself.
  • Prefer a dialog? Set Close confirmation to “Show a confirmation dialog”.

13 Emergency stop Open palm, 1.5 s

Steps
  1. Show your open palm, all five fingers spread, to the camera.
  2. Hold for 1.5 seconds - the red ring fills.
Computer action
Releases any held button and disables all gesture actions
Resuming
  • Click Resume control on the dashboard or in the tray menu, or press Ctrl+Alt+R.
  • Gestures can never resume control - only you can.
Tip
  • A brief open palm is harmless: it is also the natural “resting” pose and does nothing.

The application

Dashboard and settings pages

Screenshots of the real application (the camera image is a synthetic test hand).

System tray

Closing the window keeps the app in the tray (configurable). The tray icon dot shows the state: green = active, amber = paused, red = emergency stop. Right-click for Open dashboard, Start/Stop tracking, Pause gestures, Emergency stop and Exit. The webcam is always released on exit.

Start with Windows

Turn on Start with Windows in Settings to launch minimized to the tray when you sign in. Tracking still waits for you unless you also enable Start tracking automatically.

Safety

Safety features

Stop, pause and resume

  • Open palm for 1.5 s, the red button, the tray menu or Ctrl+Alt+X stop everything immediately and release held buttons.
  • Pause (Ctrl+Alt+P) keeps the preview running but performs no actions.
  • Losing sight of your hand for 0.25 s ends any drag or window move.

No accidental damage

  • Closing a window needs a second gesture or a dialog; the desktop, taskbar and this app can't be closed.
  • Gestures only trigger the fixed mouse/window actions listed here - never commands, scripts or typing.
  • Debouncing, hysteresis and cooldowns prevent duplicate clicks; tracking starts only when you press Start.
Help

Troubleshooting

Camera not detected
  • Open the Camera page, press Scan for cameras and pick another device number.
  • Close Teams, Zoom, OBS or the Windows Camera app - a webcam can only be used by one program.
  • Unplug and reconnect a USB webcam, then press Test camera.
MediaPipe initialization error
  • Make sure models/hand_landmarker.task exists (≈7.8 MB) - download it with the command in step 4.
  • Reinstall: pip install --force-reinstall mediapipe. Use 64-bit Python 3.10-3.13.
  • Install the latest Microsoft Visual C++ Redistributable.
Fingers not detected
  • Light your hand from the front; avoid a bright window behind you.
  • Keep the whole hand in view, 40-80 cm from the camera, palm or back facing the camera.
  • Check Controlling hand: with “Right hand” selected your left hand is ignored. Choose “Either hand” to test.
  • Lower Detection confidence on the Camera page.
Cursor shaking
  • Increase Smoothing and the Dead zone on the Cursor page.
  • Improve lighting - noise in dark images makes landmarks jitter.
  • Lower Cursor speed: a larger active area means each pixel of jitter moves the cursor less.
Accidental clicks
  • Lower the Pinch threshold (fingers must be closer) and raise Detection time for clicks.
  • Keep thumb and index apart when just pointing. A fist never clicks.
  • Raise the click Cooldown.
Double-click detection problems
  • Raise Max time between pinches (e.g. to 450 ms) and open the pinch fully between taps.
  • If single clicks feel delayed, that is the double-click window; disable Double click for instant single clicks.
Scrolling too fast (or too slow)
  • Adjust Scroll speed for vertical and horizontal scrolling separately.
  • Try the other Scroll style (touchpad vs. joystick).
Window movement not working
  • Click the window first: by default the active window moves. Or set “Window to move” to “Window under the cursor”.
  • Windows that run as administrator (Task Manager, installers) ignore non-admin apps. Run Hand Gesture Mouse as administrator if you need to control them.
  • Make sure the pinky is folded - four fingers is the swipe gesture. Check the Activity log for the exact error.
Camera permission problems
  • Windows Settings → Privacy & security → Camera: turn on Camera access and Let desktop apps access your camera.
  • Some laptops have a camera privacy shutter or a function key that disables the camera.
Application not responding
  • Press Ctrl+Alt+X to stop gesture control, then use your normal mouse.
  • Lower the camera resolution to 640 × 480 and Max tracking FPS to 20 on slow computers.
  • Run python main.py --self-test (or HandGestureMouse.exe --self-test) for a diagnostics report.
  • Check logs/app.log (Settings → Open log folder) and report the error.
  • Only one copy can run at a time - look for the tray icon.
FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Does this work without a mouse?
Yes. Moving, clicking, double-clicking, right-clicking, dragging, scrolling and window management all work by gesture. Keep a keyboard nearby for typing and for the safety hotkeys.
Does this work with a laptop webcam?
Yes. A built-in 720p webcam is plenty; tracking uses 640 × 480 by default.
Can I use an external webcam?
Yes. Pick it on the Camera page (Scan for cameras). If it already mirrors the image, turn off Mirror image.
Can I control files and folders?
Yes - double pinch opens them, pinch-and-move drags them between folders, thumb + middle pinch opens their context menu.
Can I move application windows?
Yes, with the three-finger gesture, and maximize, minimize, restore or snap them with four-finger swipes.
Can I use it with multiple monitors?
Yes. By default the active area maps onto the whole virtual desktop (all monitors). You can restrict it to the primary or any single monitor on the Cursor page.
Can I customize gestures?
Yes: enable/disable each gesture, change thresholds, hold durations, cooldowns, scroll style, the window-move gesture and every window-command mapping.
Does it work offline?
Yes. All processing happens on your computer.
Can I use it without an internet connection?
Yes. An internet connection is only needed to download the program; nothing is ever uploaded.
Is my video recorded?
No. Frames are processed in memory and discarded. Nothing is saved or sent anywhere.
